title: Winners parry less, and their parries pay more
evidence: [page:/analysis/traits]

Counting only defensive Drive Parries (leaving out the parry tap that starts a raw Drive Rush, which is how the move is input), winners parried 3.3 times a game to the loser's 3.8, and parried more in only 43% of the 145 decided sets.

What the parry led to separates them more than the count. After a winner's defensive parry, the winner landed the next hit 25% of the time and got hit 9% of the time. After a loser's, it was 18% and 12%. In about 60% of parries from either side nothing came of it.

The extra parry taps that do separate winners are the ones that start raw Drive Rush: 608 from winners against 263 from losers, the same signal as obs-0013. E.X. Dojo's Drive Parry seminar fits: parry the options you've read (target combos, Drive Rush, jump-ins, after minus moves), not as a default when you feel pressured, because a parry held into a throw or a delayed button costs Drive and the hit.
