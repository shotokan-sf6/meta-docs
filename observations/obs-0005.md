Losers used Drive Reversal about twice as often: 0.21 a game against 0.11. Most sets have none from either player; in the 49 sets where the two counts differed, the winner used more in 38%.

E.X. Dojo's Drive Reversal seminars explain why it doesn't help much: it's a guard cancel (an alpha counter), not an invincible reversal, and it's -6 if blocked. It costs 2 bars and usually only resets the situation. PepsiHeart used 11 in the FT5 against AZ_Young02 and still lost the set; struggling players reach for it when they feel stuck under pressure, when blocking (and a tech or a parry) was the cheaper answer.

Updated 2026-11-23: 122 sets (was 69). The gap narrowed (it was 0.24 vs 0.08, winner higher in 27%), so this is a lean now, not a rule. Updated 2026-11-24: 130 sets (was 122). The lean held.
