Anti-airs barely separate winners from losers (1.2 vs 1.0 a game; higher in 56% of sets), and throw techs don't at all (0.44 vs 0.46, 48%). Each can decide a set (16 air Burning Kicks went through AZ_Young02's 2HP; Cheez's Juri landed 33 Tensenrin anti-airs to the opponents' 9 across ten sets), but across the data they track the matchup and the player more than winning. Perfect parries now lean slightly toward winners (0.40 vs 0.30, 60%), up from a coin flip; worth watching as sets are added.

Composure still reads the 'wrong' way: winners score higher (3.3 vs 2.8), which means more presses after taking a big combo. The likely reason is that winners have more to do after a combo (they're ahead and pressing), so the measure may be picking up momentum rather than tilt. It needs a better definition before reviews lean on it.
